# Contributing

MNCS Rights & Provenance is an experimental specification project. Contributions should preserve the distinction between technical provenance evidence and legal conclusions.

## Contribution expectations

1. Prefer evidence-bearing, technology-neutral terminology.
2. Do not label machine-generated material as public domain merely because of its origin.
3. Do not claim that a model, agent, or tool owns copyright.
4. Identify known third-party sources and their licenses.
5. Represent uncertainty explicitly rather than filling gaps with assumptions.
6. Keep schemas interoperable and avoid coupling the specification to one model provider or runtime.
7. Add or update examples when changing schema semantics.
8. Run `python scripts/validate_examples.py` before submitting changes.

## Legal text

Do not introduce a new license, contributor agreement, or binding legal attestation as if it were settled law without explicit project review. Draft legal concepts must be clearly marked experimental/non-binding until reviewed by qualified counsel.

## Sign-off

The project may adopt the Developer Certificate of Origin or a complementary MNCS contribution attestation in a later version. Until that decision is made, contributors should ensure they have the right to submit material they contribute and should disclose material third-party provenance they know about.
